Executive Summary

As Bhutan stands on the cusp of a profound economic transformation, we at DHI are called to lead with clarity, courage, and conviction. The 10X Roadmap is our response to this national movement. It is not merely a corporate plan, it is a strategic blueprint to reimagine what DHI and the Group, and indeed Bhutan, can become over the next decade.

The challenges ahead are undeniable but it is within them that we uncover our greatest opportunities. The world is entering an era defined by exponential technological advancements, intensifying competition for resources, volatile global markets and shifting geopolitical and economic power dynamics. While our economy has long been anchored by agriculture, tourism, and hydropower and it is vital to continue nurturing these sectors, we must urgently diversify beyond hydropower. To remain competitive and resilient, Bhutan must deepen its human capital, embrace innovation, and build new capabilities to create and lead in emerging industries. This transformation requires our institutions to evolve, preserve the integrity of our national values while acquiring the agility, foresight, and executional excellence demanded by a rapidly changing global landscape.

The 10X Roadmap centers on building a robust and diversified economy through a dynamic portfolio strategy targeting five key sectors: Energy and Resources, Manufacturing and Services, Infrastructure, Mines and Minerals, and Global Securities. A key pillar of this vision is establishing a research-driven manufacturing ecosystem through the DHI InnoTech and the Jigme Namgyel Wangchuck Super Fab Lab (JNWSFL), emphasizing low-volume, high-value production.

To unlock private sector growth, DHI will invest in emerging industries and innovation, backed by a Multichannel Innovation Fund (MCIF) and strategic Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) partnerships. In the energy domain, DHI with support of the Government leads Bhutan’s goal of a 20 GW renewable portfolio while investing in future energy technologies. It also seeks to leverage Bhutan’s green energy and hydropower potential to drive an AI and Energy Economy, in partnership with GMC and GovTech.

DHI will scale its impact globally through its international subsidiaries, potentially forming strategic market linkages for Bhutanese products. At the national level, DHI is committed to fostering an innovation ecosystem by improving access to capital, shaping enabling policies, and supporting entrepreneurial growth across all stages of the innovation cycle.

Building institutions for the long term

The Roadmap is accompanied by major institutional reforms that re-architect DHI for high performance. We have introduced a professional C-suite structure, including a Chief Investment Officer, Chief Operating Officer, Chief Financial Officer, and Chief Technology Officer supported by a matrix governance system that decentralizes delivery while maintaining strategic alignment. We have also introduced the Y-Career Model, allowing high-potential talent to grow as either managers or specialists, without being forced into one-size career ladders. To institutionalize excellence, we are establishing CoEs in Project Management, ESG, R&D, and M&E.

Risk, reality, and readiness

We are clear about the risks and challenges ahead and the opportunities it provides for DHI and Bhutan at large. Overdependence on hydropower, fragility in the mining sector, weak innovation ecosystems, and talent shortages are real constraints. Global turbulence from climate volatility to digital disruption further compounds these risks. The 10X Roadmap embeds a Strategic Risk Management Framework, a transparent Monitoring & Evaluation system, and adaptive governance protocols. We are building institutions that are not only high-performing, but also resilient, ethical, and future-proof.

Destination 2035: a transformed Bhutan

By 2035, DHI’s 10X Roadmap envisions a transformed economic landscape, anchored in a Nu. 700 billion revenue base and the Nu. 30 Billion in the Bhutan Future Fund (BFF). Through strategic investments, DHI will have built globally competitive sectors, created meaningful investments and jobs, and nurtured a vibrant startup ecosystem. Agile governance and efficient portfolio management will align the DHI Group with global standards. More than growth, the 10X Roadmap represents a legacy of economic resilience, institutional confidence, and Bhutan’s emergence as a forward-looking, innovation-driven nation.

Seven strategic focus areas

1. Strengthening corporate governance and accountability

Robust governance is the cornerstone of long-term value creation. DHI is committed to upholding the highest standards of corporate governance across its portfolio. This includes enhancing the capacity and effectiveness of subsidiary boards, instituting clear governance frameworks, and promoting strategic autonomy balanced by transparent oversight. Through structured performance compacts and regular strategic reviews, DHI ensures that each company is evaluated not only on financial outcomes, but also on strategic alignment, innovation capacity, and national contribution.

2. Driving operational efficiency across the portfolio

Operational efficiency is a strategic necessity. DHI will promote group-wide operational improvements through systems integration, lean process redesign, and shared services in core functions such as finance, human resources, procurement, and digital infrastructure. These efforts are designed to eliminate redundancies, reduce costs, and improve service delivery across the board. Efficiency gains will be reinvested into innovation, talent development, and customer excellence.

3. Enhancing capital allocation and financial efficiency

Resources will be channeled towards companies and sectors that demonstrate long-term growth potential, strategic relevance, and alignment with national priorities. This logic will prioritize entities in the Potential, Growth, and Future Zones, guided by criteria such as return on investment, capital productivity, and national economic contribution. DHI will also pursue strategic divestments from non-core, underperforming, or legacy assets. These are deliberate reallocations of national capital, from static holdings to dynamic opportunities, with proceeds reinvested in digital infrastructure, frontier industries, and knowledge-based enterprises.

4. Institutionalizing enterprise risk management and resilience planning

DHI will reinforce and institutionalize its Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework across the Group, including regular stress testing, scenario planning, and dynamic risk assessments to address sector-specific vulnerabilities, particularly in areas like hydropower, which are exposed to climate volatility and geopolitical uncertainties. In parallel, DHI will pursue greater sectoral diversification, especially in green technologies such as solar and smart energy solutions.

5. Driving innovation and enabling digital transformation

The Group will embed a culture of innovation across its companies by encouraging investment in R&D, incentivizing experimentation, and nurturing intrapreneurship. Digital transformation will be a core focus, particularly in finance, telecom, and energy, where blockchain, 5G, AI-driven optimization, and smart grids offer game-changing potential. Companies will be encouraged to reengineer their business models using digital identity systems, automation, and next-generation analytics.

6. Mainstreaming environmental, social and governance (ESG) commitments

In alignment with Bhutan’s philosophy of Gross National Happiness (GNH) and its sustainability commitments, DHI will adopt a Group-wide ESG framework. This will involve updating its Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) guidelines and embedding ESG metrics into corporate evaluations, guiding subsidiaries toward environmentally sustainable practices, social inclusion, and stronger governance standards.

7. Deepening stakeholder engagement and transparency

Transparency and trust are foundational to DHI’s legitimacy as a public institution. The Group will institutionalize robust financial and operational reporting systems across all subsidiaries. Disclosures to the Government, regulators, and the public will be timely, accurate, and consistent. DHI will also prioritize two-way engagement with employees, citizens, policymakers, and private partners.

DHI’s Portfolio Management Strategy is not simply about improving performance, it is about reimagining Bhutan’s economic core to meet the demands of the 21st century.

Pillar II: Investment Strategy

Channeling capital into engines of transformation for national growth and global relevance.

To realise Bhutan’s 10X economic aspiration, DHI is implementing a focused, and future-facing Investment Strategy. Anchored in Bhutan’s comparative advantages, its natural capital, sovereign prerogatives, and geo-economic/political potential, this strategy targets high-growth, high-impact sectors of the future that can reshape the national economy, create jobs, and attract global partnerships. In parallel, DHI has established the BFF to safeguard resources for future generations and respond to national contingencies. DHI aims to grow the BFF to Nu. 30 billion over the next decade.

In close collaboration with the Royal Government, the GMC initiative, private partners, and international allies, DHI will steer investments into five key sectors: Energy and Resources, Mines and Minerals, Infrastructure, Manufacturing and Services, and Global Securities.

Strategic objectives

  • Implement the DHI Triangulation Strategy: develop a green energy portfolio in partnership with strategic global allies to power next-generation clean energy–intensive industries, such as AI data centers. It aligns three pillars: energy investment partners, industrial investment partners, and the combined role of DHI and the Royal Government in providing enabling policies, regulatory support, and catalytic capital.
  • Establish long-term economic engines by prioritizing sectors with exponential potential: energy systems, critical mineral exploration, digital infrastructure, advanced low volume high value manufacturing, and wellness services.
  • Mobilize global capital and expertise by developing commercially bankable projects and ensuring DHI serves as a credible gateway for global and local investors.
  • De-risk the national economy through geographical and sectoral diversification.
  • Strengthen sovereign investment capability by professionalizing asset management, developing structured financial products, and building internal capacity for global market engagement.

1. Energy and Resources

Energy resources

Bhutan has a hydroelectric potential of estimated 36 GW and an installed capacity of 3.6 GW as of 2025. However, the firm power available is only around 700 MW, while the peak load is already beyond 1,000 MW and is expected to exceed 1,500 MW by 2025 end. Of the approximately 12,000 MU of energy generated in 2024, 50% is consumed domestically, with the remainder exported to India. However, Bhutan imports over 1,000 plus MU annually during the lean winter months. The energy system must address both the peak power demands at specific times and the base load energy requirements during the 6-month deficit period each year. Currently, this deficit is met through the day-ahead market and some long-term reimport strategies from India.

Bhutan’s 2040 energy plan includes an additional 15 GW of hydroelectric power and 5 GW of solar power, necessitating an estimated investment of over 26 billion USD. Given climate change, hydropower risks, rising construction costs and the levelized cost of energy (LCOE), it is essential to implement a national energy security investment plan with a diversified generation portfolio. Beyond conventional hydro, the green portfolio approach integrates solar, geothermal (shallow, traditional and advanced), hydrogen and combined cycle, pumped storage, grid scale batteries and energy storage, exploration of natural hydrogen and small modular reactors (SMR) for base load as technology matures. Investments will also target smart grid and electricity market mechanisms across generation, transmission and distribution.

Energy as an enabler through a triangulation strategy: with rapid advances in material science, energy and storage technologies and quantum computing, accelerated by the AI revolution, a window exists to strategize for the demand of clean power-intensive industries (viz. AI data centres). The “triangulation framework” brings together (a) global energy investors, (b) global industrial partners (specifically AI data centre and hyper-scalers), and (c) Government, Private Sector and DHI. It would help build a robust domestic market ecosystem for energy, create the conditions for Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs), and enable new industry verticals.

Forest resources

With over 70% of Bhutan’s land under forest cover, the country is uniquely positioned to lead a timber innovation economy. DHI will build an integrated ecosystem for engineered timber production (e.g., Glulam, CLT), supporting GMC’s sustainable construction goals and creating high-value export potential. This includes design and production facilities, circular forestry systems, carbon markets (e.g., HFLD credits), and frontier R&D in materials science and wood engineering, integrated with the flagship project of Nature Capital Accounting (NCA).

Water resources

Water is both a lifeline and an economic opportunity. DHI will study water as a strategic resource and industry with global partners, positioning Bhutan’s water as an economic driver beyond hydropower while ensuring long-term water security. Studies will explore water storage potential by developing natural catchment areas, and investment in safe drinking water, purification technologies, IoT-enabled water monitoring, fresh water monitoring and tokenization, and tourism and community initiatives like water parks, backed by a strong R&D thrust.

2. Mines and Minerals

DHI will work closely with MoENR to unlock Bhutan’s untapped mineral potential sustainably and pursue commercialization. Using modern geological mapping, AI, drone technology, and international best practices, the focus is on exploring and adding value to magnesium, graphite, tungsten, lithium, copper, molybdenum, and rare earth elements. At present, SMCL mines coal, gypsum, and dolomite, and DHI is exploring ways to increase their value through processing and innovation. DHI plans to expand to both metallic and non-metallic minerals critical for emerging industries.

This sector will be a major driver of 10X growth, contributing to economic diversification, foreign exchange stability, export competitiveness, and downstream industrial development. Strategic partnerships and sustainable, responsible mining practices will be essential for transparency and long-term national benefit.

3. Infrastructure: Physical, Digital, and Financial

Physical infrastructure

As the government’s investment arm, DHI must strategically prioritize infrastructure that fosters technological advancement, business opportunity, and connectivity. With GMC positioned as a gateway between South and Southeast Asia via railways, airways, and other multimodal networks, seamless multimodal connectivity between GMC and Bhutan’s major cities is imperative. With the international airport at GMC and advanced discussions on connecting GMC and Samtse to Indian Railway systems, DHI will plan next-generation urban and inter-city infrastructure, including:

  • Advanced transportation (electric/hydrogen vehicles, underground transport, drone logistics, flying car testing, cable and ropeways);
  • Future smart cities (Phuentsholing township, special economic zones); and
  • HPC clusters powered by Bhutan’s clean energy, pursued through the triangulation strategy with GMC.

Through Drukair, DHI will explore key aviation support services at Gelephu International Airport: catering, ground handling, engineering and maintenance, and a certified Aircraft Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul (MRO) facility, transforming Gelephu into an integrated aerotropolis.

Digital infrastructure

DHI will expand the connectivity backbone through fiber expansion, last-mile Fiber To The Home (FTTH) rollout, and satellite-ground link integration with GovTech Agency. Bhutan’s national backbone runs on Optical Ground Wire (OPGW), with All-Dielectric Self-Supporting (ADSS) cable linking each of the 205 gewogs via India. GMC plans to expand dark fiber connectivity to subsea cables, and Starlink was introduced in 2024. The strategy includes supporting GMC connectivity, FTTH deployment, evolving wireless systems, and 5G and beyond to foster an IoT economy.

Financial infrastructure

The National Digital Identity (NDI) could be the backbone of financial infrastructure. DHI will expand Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) through decentralized NDI systems built on Self Sovereign Identity principles, extending NDI use cases to government services, utilities, the corporate sector, single sign on, digital/cryptographic signatures, e-KYC and more.

4. Manufacturing and Services

Manufacturing

Manufacturing has stagnated at about 7% of GDP since 2011, well below the 18% needed for high-income status. DHI will pursue a ‘low volume, high value’ strategy: design, engineering and manufacturing of efficient EV motors, humanoid robot components, and space industry technologies aligned with GovTech’s mission. The Group is investing in a geothermal spa, mass-engineered timber, energy-efficient charcoal and a magnesium production plant. Beyond cement and steel, DHI will explore logistics, EV assembly and manufacturing, battery and solar panel assembly, and the semiconductor industry, building a manufacturing and service strategy for a regional market of over 2 billion people.

Services

Tourism, health, education, logistics and aviation services hold significant potential. DHI will explore premium medical and wellness services; education-tech and transnational education campuses; and logistics and aviation services including catering, security, and airport management, leveraging Bhutan’s soft power, institutional trust, and natural environment.

5. Global Securities and Digital Assets

Global financial securities: DHI will gradually expand exposure to global capital markets through managed portfolios of equities, fixed income, and thematic funds built via strategic partnerships, and expand assets under management.

Digital assets: DHI will maintain a balanced portfolio considering digital assets as a store of value and as opportunities in tokenomics, crypto currencies, CBDC, Blockchain and Web3. Tokenized infrastructure, programmable money, and decentralised applications will be monitored and selectively engaged through a sandbox model with regulators and global innovation partners.
